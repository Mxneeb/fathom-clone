# Fathom Competitive Teardown

Research conducted 2026-09-13 by browsing the live product at fathom.video (redirects to fathom.ai) using a real Google account login. This document is built incrementally, flow by flow, as each part of the product is explored live in Chrome.

**Honesty note on live-call testing:** Anywhere this doc describes "getting the notetaker into a meeting," I explicitly state whether I actually ran a real Zoom/Meet/Teams call to observe it, or am inferring from in-app copy/settings screens/marketing content. Do not read anything as observed unless labeled so.

## 1. Landing page

- URL `fathom.video` redirects to `https://www.fathom.ai/`.
- Dark, space-themed marketing page. Top banner: "IT'S A WHOLE NEW ORBIT. ANNOUNCING BOT-FREE CAPTURE. A NEW DESKTOP APP, CHATGPT & CLAUDE INTEGRATIONS & MORE" — Fathom is clearly leading with **bot-free capture** as its newest differentiator (a direct response to Zoom/Meet cracking down on bot-join friction, and to user annoyance at visible notetaker bots in calls).
- Hero copy: "Fathom summarizes your meetings so you can focus on the conversation. **Now available bot-free.**"
- Primary CTA: "GET STARTED – FREE FOREVER" (blue pill button) — free tier framed as permanent, not a trial, which is a deliberate trust/growth-loop bet (compare to competitors that lead with a 14-day trial).
- Trust badges directly under the CTA: SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, HIPAA Compliant, SSO/SCIM — aimed squarely at enterprise/IT buyers evaluating a notetaker bot that has access to sensitive calls.
- Hero visual shows a mini capture-mode picker: "Audio & video / Audio [BOT-FREE] / Transcript only [BOT-FREE] / Capture off" — this is a preview of a real in-product control (confirmed later in settings).
- Cookie consent modal appears on load (Customize / Reject All / Accept All) — clicked **Accept All** to proceed.

Screenshot: `01-landing-page.png`

**Product bet noted:** Leading the entire homepage with "bot-free" signals Fathom sees visible-bot fatigue (and platform pushback) as the single biggest UX complaint in this category right now, and is repositioning around it before competitors do.

## 2. Sign-up flow (observed live, real Google account)

Clicked "Get Started – Free Forever" → `fathom.video/users/sign_up`.

- **Step 1 — auth method.** Only two options shown: **Continue with Google** and **Continue with Microsoft**. No email/password path at all — Fathom fully outsources identity to work-email SSO providers. This simplifies signup to one click but also means there is no "just try it with any email" path; you must have a Google or Microsoft account. Screenshot: `02-signup-choice.png`.
- **Step 2 — Google account picker,** standard Google chrome, not Fathom's UI. Screenshot: `03-google-oauth-calendar-checkbox` shows the *first* OAuth consent screen: only `name/email/profile` requested here, **not** calendar — that's deliberately deferred to a second, separate OAuth round-trip.
- **Step 3 — personal vs. work email fork.** Because the test account (`farums1976@gmail.com`) looked personal, Fathom interstitially asked: *"Are your meetings on your company calendar? We recommend using your work email."* Two cards: **Personal use only** ("Suited for one-off calls," view only your own meetings, no shared workspace, **and explicitly: "Cannot convert to a team plan later"**) vs. **Individual or Team use** (badged "RECOMMENDED," "Most powerful features & insights," set individual/team prefs, shared workspaces, add teammates anytime). Screenshot: `05-onboarding-personal-vs-work-email.png`.
  - **Product bet:** this is a monetization/expansion safeguard disguised as a UX nicety — funneling personal-gmail signups toward "Individual or Team" (i.e. billable-seat trajectory) and explicitly punishing the "personal" choice by making it a dead end (no later upgrade path). A user who picks "Personal" to move fast is quietly locked out of ever becoming a team seat on the same account.
- **Step 4 — calendar OAuth, round 2.** A dedicated interstitial: *"Next, Fathom needs to know of your upcoming video meetings"* with a single **Connect to Calendar** CTA and reassurance copy: *"Fathom has only read access to your calendar. It cannot modify or add events."* (Screenshot `04-onboarding-connect-calendar.png`). Clicking it re-triggers Google OAuth, this time asking for the `calendar.events.readonly` scope specifically, on its own consent screen (screenshot `03-google-oauth-calendar-checkbox.png` — the underlying page even showed an *unchecked* optional checkbox for this scope during the very first OAuth grant, meaning Fathom's flow supports a user declining calendar at first ask, but then simply asks again immediately as a mandatory-feeling next step with no visible skip).
- **Step 5 — auto-record preferences.** *"Take notes on [ALL MEETINGS IN MY CALENDAR ▾] and share with [ALL ATTENDEES ▾]"*. Dropdown options:
  - "Take notes on": **All meetings in my calendar** (tagged "Most Common," the default) vs. **Don't automate. I'll capture manually** (tagged "App Download Needed"). Screenshot `07-onboarding-auto-record-dropdown.png`.
  - "Share with": **All attendees** (tagged **"Recommended for GDPR"**, the default) vs. **Only me**. Screenshot `08-onboarding-share-with-dropdown.png`.
  - A mandatory checkbox: *"I understand I'm responsible for collecting attendee consent for the recording and transcription, in accordance with applicable laws."* — Continue is disabled until checked. Fathom explicitly pushes legal liability for consent-gathering onto the user, while badging its own default ("all attendees") as the GDPR-safe choice.
  - **Default behavior is maximally aggressive:** out of the box, Fathom will silently auto-join and record *every single meeting on your calendar* and auto-share the summary with *every attendee*, including external ones, unless you go out of your way to change it during this 5-second decision. That's a strong, deliberate product bet — favoring engagement/virality (external attendees get a taste of Fathom in every meeting) over a conservative default.
- **Step 6 — demographic questionnaire** (`10-onboarding-role-dropdown.png`, `11-onboarding-crm-dropdown.png`): "I work in [Department] as [Role] and use [CRM]." Departments: Sales, Customer Success, Product/Design, Marketing, Operations, Engineering, Other. Roles: individual contributor, manager, executive. CRM field only appears *after* department is chosen (dynamic form) — offers HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Close, Microsoft, Attio, Other, "I don't use a CRM." This is pure segmentation data for sales/marketing — none of it visibly changes the product experience later, but it lets Fathom's GTM team route sales-flagged signups (Sales dept + has a CRM) to outbound follow-up.
- **Step 7 — intended use** (`12-onboarding-intended-use.png`): "By Myself" (Summaries and action items for your meetings) vs. "With My Team" (Share and organize meetings in one place) — a second, softer team-vs-solo fork, reinforcing the upsell path from step 3.
- **Step 8 — Connect to Zoom** (`13-onboarding-connect-zoom.png`): *"Zoom requires a connection for Fathom to capture your meetings."* CTA "Connect to Zoom," plus reassurance: *"Don't worry, Fathom will only join the meetings that you ask it to. You're in control here."* **This step has an explicit skip link — "Never join Zoom meetings? Skip this step."** Notably this is the *only* connector offered at this point in onboarding; Google Meet and Microsoft Teams are not mentioned here at all (they're likely handled automatically via calendar detection + browser/desktop-level join, not a discrete OAuth connection like Zoom's app-marketplace model requires). I skipped this since I don't have Zoom OAuth credentials for the test account.
- **Step 9 — Install Desktop App, and this is a hard gate on Windows** (`14-onboarding-desktop-app-download.png`). Two options shown:
  - **Fathom Classic** — "Capture, transcribe and summarize every meeting with the current Fathom app" — Download for Windows (a plain NSIS/Electron installer, `Fathom-1.42.6 Setup.exe`, hosted on Fathom's own Google Cloud Storage bucket, not through the Microsoft Store).
  - **Fathom Bot-Free Capture** — "No bot joining your calls. Fathom captures meeting quietly in the background." — labeled **"Coming Soon to Windows"**, button reads "Notify Me" only.
  - **This is a real, load-bearing finding, not a minor detail: the entire "bot-free" headline pitch on the homepage is not actually shipped for Windows.** Windows users are funneled straight back into the classic bot-join Electron app. There is no visible skip/"do this later" control on this screen — navigating directly to `fathom.video/`, `fathom.video/calls`, or `app.fathom.video/calls` all hard-redirected back to `fathom.video/zoomless/onboarding/download` until the desktop app was actually installed. This is the single most aggressive gate in the whole funnel: everything past this point (dashboard, recordings, summaries, sharing) is unreachable from a fresh account without installing native software.
  - I downloaded and ran the official installer (`Fathom-1.42.6 Setup.exe` from `storage.googleapis.com/electron_releases/...`) to get past this gate, per explicit user approval — this is disclosed here because it's a real system-level install, not just a browser click.

**Product bets in onboarding, summarized:** Fathom's onboarding is long (9 discrete steps) but almost every step has a pre-selected "recommended" default that biases toward (a) team/paid-seat trajectory, (b) maximal auto-record/auto-share behavior, and (c) calendar+CRM data collection for GTM — while wrapping each of those defaults in reassuring, compliance-flavored copy ("read-only," "GDPR recommended," "you're in control"). The one place friction is allowed to kill conversion is the Zoom step (skippable) — everything that gates the *core* product (calendar, desktop app) is not.

## 4. Settings — the richest single screen in the product

`fathom.video/customize` (via the top-nav **Settings** gear). This one page holds nearly every configuration surface in the product.

**Top-of-page global preference bar** — same "Auto-record [ALL MEETINGS ▾] and auto-share [SUMMARY & RECORDING ▾] with attendees" control seen in onboarding, now editable anytime.

**Video Conferencing** (screenshot `24-settings-video-conferencing.png`) — this is the direct answer to "how does the notetaker get into a meeting":
- **Zoom: "Partially Enabled"** (amber) — "Connect to Zoom account for more reliable recording" + Connect button. Sub-options: "Auto-capture unscheduled Zoom meetings" (off by default) and **"Disable 'Recording in progress' audio notification"** with a "↗ Update in Zoom Settings" deep link — notable because Fathom *cannot* silence Zoom's own audio announcement from its own settings panel; it has to hand you off to Zoom's settings to do it, meaning by default every Zoom participant hears an audible "this meeting is being recorded" notice unless the host separately configures Zoom itself.
- **Google Meet: "Partially Enabled"** (amber) — "Usage limited to scheduled calls joined via desktop app. Install Chrome extension to use on any meeting." + Install Chrome Extension button, plus "Auto-capture unscheduled Google Meet meetings" toggle (off). So the desktop app alone only catches Meet calls that are on your calendar; ad-hoc/unscheduled Meet calls need the Chrome extension too.
- **Microsoft Teams: "Fully Enabled"** (green) — no connect button, no extra install needed — works out of the box, unlike Zoom and Meet. (I did not verify this live with a real Teams call; it's the settings page's own status label, not something I observed joining a call.)

**Premium Features** (`28-settings-premium-features.png`) — banner: *"🎁 30 DAYS LEFT IN FREE PREVIEW: UPGRADE NOW - LEARN MORE"* (this test account is inside a full-featured trial window, which is why premium toggles below are live/editable even though the account is nominally "Personal/Free"):
- **Bot Name** — "Professional's Fathom Notetaker" (auto-generated from the Google account's display name), editable — this is literally the name every other participant sees join the call as a bot.
- **Auto-Generate Action Items** (tagged RECOMMENDED, **on**) — "Fathom AI will automatically extract any action items discussed on your meetings."
- **Default Meeting Summary Template** — dropdown, set to **Enhanced**, "External meetings only. Attendees always see the Enhanced template if you share." (Full template list captured below in §6.)
- **Recording Notification Banner** (tagged PREVIEW, **on**) — "WARNING: If you disable this you're required to collect recording consent according to the laws of you and your attendees' jurisdictions." A visible on-screen recording banner is the default, and disabling it explicitly shifts legal risk to the user.

**Options** (`29-settings-options-consent-sharing.png`):
- **Auto Request Recording Consent** (tagged RECOMMENDED, but **off** by default — the one place a "recommended" toggle isn't pre-enabled) — "Fathom collects recording consent from attendees of external calls in advance so you don't have to." A real automated consent-collection mechanism exists, it's just not turned on out of the box, which slightly undercuts the "you're responsible for consent" liability language shown at signup.
- **Default Share Link Access** — dropdown, default **"Anyone with the link can view."** Public-by-default link sharing is a meaningful default worth flagging from a security/privacy standpoint.
- **In-meeting Chat Interface** (on) — "Allows users to request meeting summaries from within a call **and displays your referral link which gets you free premium features as users join.**" This embeds a referral/growth loop directly inside live meetings — every guest in a call sees an in-meeting chat surface that doubles as a referral ad for the host's account.
- **"Use my anonymized data to improve Fathom's AI for everyone"** (on by default) — opt-out, not opt-in, with a "Learn More" link.

**Fathom Apps** (`29`/`30` screenshots): Desktop App (tagged RECOMMENDED, "Configure" since already installed), Chrome Extension ("Companion app for those who use Google Meet" — Install), Zoom App ("Only available when you're the meeting host" — Install).

**Highlight Options** (`30-settings-highlight-options-delete-account.png`) — the taxonomy behind the highlighting feature. Four default tags, each color-swatched: **HIGHLIGHT** (blue, the base tag, not deletable), **POSITIVE REACTION** (green), **NEEDS REVIEW** (yellow), **FEEDBACK** (orange) — the latter three are reorderable (drag handles) and deletable (trash icons), plus a **"+ ADD MORE"** control to define custom tags. This means highlighting isn't a single generic bookmark — it's a small configurable tagging taxonomy, presumably so teams can build workflows like "flag anything tagged NEEDS REVIEW into a QA playlist."

**Delete Account** — plain danger-zone block: "Deleting your account is permanent. All recordings and data will be deleted." + red "Delete Account" button. No extra confirmation step observed on this screen itself (didn't click through to avoid actually deleting the test account).

**Account menu** (avatar, top-right — `31-account-menu-dropdown.png`): Start Test Call, Tutorial, FAQs, Developers, Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, Security & Compliance, System Status, Download App, Logout, plus "Logged in as [email]". **No Billing/Plan link lives in this menu** — billing is reached only via the "Upgrade Now" link inside Settings' Premium Features banner.

**Integrations** (`25-settings-integrations.png`): Claude ("Ask anything about your meetings"), ChatGPT (same), Zapier ("Automate sending Fathom content to almost any app"), Slack ("Automatically send highlights to Slack in real-time"), Salesforce ("Sync call summaries & highlights to matching Contacts, Accounts, and open Opportunities"), HubSpot (visible but cut off in the scroll, same CRM-sync framing). Every integration row has its own "Connect" button — nothing is bundled or auto-connected.

## 5. Pricing & free-tier limits (from Settings → Upgrade Now, and the public pricing page)

In-app upgrade screen (`fathom.video/manage_subscription`, screenshots `32`/`33`): shows **Current Plan: Fathom - Free**, with a Monthly/Annual toggle (Annual saves ~20%: **$16/mo** billed annually vs **$20/mo** billed monthly) for **Fathom Premium**, listing: Advanced AI Summary Templates, AI Action Items, AI-generated Follow-up Emails, Bot Naming. "100% money-back guarantee," plus a cross-link to "Fathom Team Edition" for multi-seat plans.

| Plan | Price | Included |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** (individual) | $0 forever | Unlimited recordings + transcriptions; choice of bot-free (beta) or bot capture; instant AI call summaries; clips, playlists + search across calls |

Other pricing-page details: a 90-day money-back guarantee banner on every paid tier; growth offers for "Qualified Portfolio Program" (VC-backed startups get 2 years of Fathom Team free), "10 free seats for nonprofits," and a pointed **"Switching from Gong?"** callout offering free Fathom Business "through your contract" plus free data migration — Fathom is explicitly conquest-marketing against Gong (and, per the `fathom.ai/solutions/teams` page banner, Fireflies too).

## 6. Post-call flows — INFERRED, NOT OBSERVED

**Honesty checkpoint:** everything above (sections 1–5) was captured live, in this session, in the actual product with a real Google account and a real desktop-app install. Everything in this section was **not**. I was explicitly asked to stop short of running a real Zoom/Meet/Teams call (the account has no recordings, and Fathom ships no sample/demo recording for a fresh account — the in-app "Start Test Call" option itself requires a connected Zoom account, which this test account doesn't have). Everything below is reconstructed from Fathom's own marketing pages (`fathom.ai/overview`), its public help center (`help.fathom.video`), and third-party reviews (cited inline), and should be read as *credible secondhand description*, not firsthand observation. Where a source is a third-party review rather than Fathom's own docs, I say so.

### 6.1 Live meeting state (inferred)
Per Fathom's own overview page: a **live summary updates in real time as the conversation happens**, so a user who tunes out can catch up without waiting for the call to end. There's also a **private scratchpad** for personal notes during the call, with the option to tag teammates so collaboration can start mid-meeting rather than only after. The desktop app additionally provides an **in-meeting chat interface** (confirmed from this session's own Settings page, §4 above) where the bot itself can be asked questions live ("Ask Fathom" from inside the call), and which — per that same settings toggle — displays the host's referral link to other participants as they join. I could not observe what this actually looks like on-screen during a call.

### 6.2 Post-call playback & transcript sync (inferred, third-party-review-supported)
Per Fathom's help center and multiple independent reviews: the transcript on the call-detail page is **fully clickable** — clicking any sentence in the transcript jumps video playback to that exact moment, and clicking an item in the AI summary does the same (jumps to the relevant point in the recording rather than requiring manual scrubbing). One third-party long-term reviewer (tldv.io's comparative review) reported a **timestamp-accuracy bug on very long calls** — a 2–3 hour Zoom call had transcript timestamps compressed as if the call were only ~6 minutes long. I cannot confirm this is still true; it's flagged here as a known-reported edge case, not a verified one.

### 6.3 AI summary & templates in practice (inferred)
Section 4 already captured the full template picklist (Enhanced, Sales, Sales-Sandler, Sales-SPICED, Sales-MEDDPICC, Sales-BANT, Customer Success, Customer Success-REACH, Candidate Interview, Demo, One-on-One, Project Kick-Off, Project Update, Q&A, Retrospective, Stand Up) directly from the live Settings page. Per Fathom's help docs, templates aren't fixed once generated: on a given recording you can **swap the template and click "Regenerate Summary"** to redo it in the new structure, refine the prompt behind any template via a pencil/edit icon, revert to the template's default via an arrow control, and optionally **"Apply to Future Summaries"** to make a one-off tweak the new default for that template going forward. Structurally, the different templates are described (by their own subtitle copy seen live) as changing what the AI is told to extract — e.g. Sales-BANT/MEDDPICC/SPICED/Sandler each impose a different sales-methodology skeleton on the same transcript, Customer Success-REACH imposes an "expansion framework," Candidate Interview reframes the same call around "a candidate's experience, goals, and responses," Stand Up around "daily progress, tasks, and obstacles." I was not able to generate a real summary to see the literal rendered section headers, so I can't confirm exact output structure — only that the template picker actively reframes what gets extracted, per Fathom's own subtitle copy.

### 6.4 Action items (inferred)
A Premium+ feature (§5). Per Fathom's help documentation: action items support **assigning an owner and a due date** directly from the recording view, and there's a direct **"Sync to Asana"** button on the call-recording page's action-items block for one-click push (no manual re-entry) — the same sync pattern presumably applies to the other integrations seen live in Settings (Zapier, Salesforce, HubSpot). I could not confirm whether action items support an in-app checkbox/mark-complete state distinct from syncing out to a task tool.

### 6.5 Highlighting → where it surfaces (inferred, cross-checked against what I did observe)
This session did directly observe the *destination*: the live Playlists tab (§3) describes itself as exactly where saved highlights end up, organized into shareable collections, and the live Settings page (§4) showed the actual highlight-tag taxonomy (HIGHLIGHT / POSITIVE REACTION / NEEDS REVIEW / FEEDBACK, customizable). Per Fathom's help docs and third-party write-ups: hitting "Highlight" during a live call (or while scrubbing a past recording afterward) flags that moment, Fathom auto-generates a short clip and a written summary of just that moment, timestamps it, and inserts it into the meeting's notes — from there it can be pulled into a Playlist for cross-meeting organization (e.g., a "customer objections" reel or an onboarding clip-reel) or sent straight to Slack in real time (the Slack integration seen live in Settings is described specifically as "Automatically send highlights to Slack in real-time").

### 6.6 Search — what's actually indexed (inferred)
Two distinct search surfaces exist, matching what was visible live in the top nav (a "Search Call Recordings" box) and the right-hand "Ask Fathom" panel:
- **Literal keyword search** ("Search Call Recordings"): per Fathom's help docs, this searches transcript text for a keyword, phrase, name, or email address, and lets you click "Play" next to a result to jump straight to that timestamp in that call. Per Fathom's transcript data model, each transcript line carries the speaker's display name plus their matched calendar-invitee email, so speaker names are part of what's searchable/indexed, not just raw dialogue.
- **"Ask Fathom" (conversational/account-wide search)**: a separate, LLM-backed layer that answers natural-language questions ("what follow-ups did I commit to this week?") across every meeting a user (or, on Team plans, a whole org) has had, and — per Fathom's own marketing copy — returns answers "with citations that link to the exact moment in every transcript," i.e. it's explicitly designed to ground its answers in retrievable transcript spans rather than free-floating summarization.

### 6.7 Sharing — the recipient-side experience (inferred)
Section 4 already showed the account-level default (**"Anyone with the link can view"**) live in Settings. Per Fathom's help center: sharing a specific recording exposes the same three access tiers at the per-recording level — **Anyone with the link**, **Anyone on your same domain**, or **Only people added** — and a recipient who was never on the call does **not** need a Fathom account to open a shared link; they land on a page with the recording, transcript, and the AI summary/"questions." Access is tiered: a "Limited" recipient sees transcript + summary + recording only; a "Standard" recipient additionally sees highlights, action items, can download the call, and (on Team plans) leave comments; an "Admin"-tier recipient can further trim the recording or edit the transcript. Separately, and more consequentially: **auto-share (the default this test account enabled during onboarding) goes out to every person on the calendar invite, even those who didn't actually attend the meeting** — per Fathom's own help docs, this happens automatically right after the call ends, and once sent, **the email itself cannot be recalled** (though individual link access can be revoked afterward). This is a real, sharp edge worth flagging: a calendar invitee who no-shows, or was cc'd on an invite they never planned to join, can still receive the recording and AI summary automatically.

### 6.8 Team / multi-person calls — how an 8-person hour-long call differs (inferred)
I could not run a real multi-person long call to observe this directly. What's confirmed from Fathom's own marketing/product copy: transcription is explicitly built to handle "multiple speakers, accents, cross-talk, languages, and side conversations" with per-speaker attribution carried through even in bot-free capture modes. The Team-tier upsell screen I did see live (§3, `19-team-calls-paywall.png`) shows the shape of what team-scale analytics look like once a call library has real volume: per-rep **"Talk Time %"** and **"Monologues & Questions"** coaching metrics, and an org-wide searchable call library ("Your team's customer calls all in one (searchable) place"). Fathom's own help center on the Team Call Library confirms filtering by team/keyword and per-call visibility controls (locked / team-shared / all-teams), but its documentation doesn't itself describe duration- or speaker-count-driven UI changes — so whatever difference exists between an 8-person hour-long call and a 2-person 5-minute call is most likely expressed only in (a) transcript length and the summary's ability to compress it, (b) the number of distinct speaker labels and per-speaker talk-time stats, and (c) (per the timestamp-accuracy complaint noted in §6.2) a greater risk of drift/compression bugs in very long recordings — not in a materially different UI shell.

## What I'd build first

Ranked by how much each flow matters to the *core felt experience* of "I had a meeting, and now something valuable exists because of it" — not by how impressive it would be to demo.

**1. Transcript ⟷ playback sync, with a clickable transcript and clickable summary items jumping to timestamp.** This is the single UI moment that makes the product feel real rather than like "a summary email." Everything else is content sitting on top of this spine. Build for real, first.

**2. AI summary generation, with 2–3 templates (not 16).** The AI summary is the actual value prop; the *choice* of template is a monetization lever more than a UX necessity — Fathom itself gates 12+ of its 16 templates behind Premium and gives Free users only the baseline. I'd build one solid general-purpose template for real, plus one or two of the highest-leverage niche ones (Sales, maybe Stand-Up), and treat the rest of the picklist as "add more prompts later" rather than a day-one requirement.

**3. Action item extraction.** High perceived value for very little marginal build cost once summary generation exists (it's the same LLM call, different extraction target) — no reason not to build this for real from day one, even on a stubbed capture layer.

**4. Search — keyword-in-transcript, built for real; "Ask Fathom"-style cross-meeting Q&A, stubbed or deferred.** Full-text search over stored transcripts is cheap and high-value. A RAG-style conversational assistant across every meeting is a genuinely harder system (retrieval, citation-grounding, multi-meeting context) — worth a fake/scripted demo response in 24 hours, not a real implementation.

**5. Highlighting, in a lightweight form.** Build the tag-a-timestamp-with-a-note mechanic for real (it's cheap: a timestamp + label + short text). **Fake the auto-clip video generation** (trimming/re-encoding video server-side is real infra work with little payoff for a 24-hour build) — a "highlight" can just deep-link into the full recording at that timestamp instead of producing a standalone clip file.

**6. Sharing, minimally.** Build a real share link with one access tier (`anyone with the link`) — skip the three-tier permission system and the auto-share-to-every-calendar-invitee automation entirely for v1. That automation is a real product bet, not a baseline expectation.

**7. The capture/bot-joins-a-live-call layer — fake this entirely, and don't apologize for it.** This is the one piece of Fathom's product that is genuinely hard infrastructure: joining a live Zoom/Meet/Teams call as a bot (or capturing bot-free), handling real-time audio, and running streaming ASR. It is also, tellingly, the one place **Fathom fakes it for its own new users** — the "Start Test Call... don't worry, she's just a recording" onboarding CTA (§3) exists precisely because Fathom doesn't expect a fresh signup to have a real call ready either. I'd copy that instinct directly: ship a "record something" entry point that's actually just an upload-a-video-or-audio-file flow (or 2–3 canned sample transcripts a user can pick from), and spend zero of the 24 hours on real meeting-platform bot infrastructure. Everything downstream (transcript, summary, action items, highlights, search, sharing) can then be built and demoed for real against that stubbed input.

**8. Live in-meeting state (real-time summary/chat while the call is happening) — skip for v1.** This requires a live streaming-transcription pipeline running concurrently with an in-progress call, which is materially harder than post-call batch processing for very little additional perceived value in a first build. Note it as a fast-follow, not a gap to apologize for.

**9. Onboarding ceremony (personal-vs-work-email fork, calendar OAuth, demographic questionnaire, CRM question, desktop-app gate) — build the absolute minimum shell, fake the rest.** Real Google OAuth for login is cheap and worth doing for real (it's one library call). Everything past that — calendar sync, auto-record scheduling rules, the multi-step "tell us about yourself" funnel — is GTM/segmentation infrastructure that has nothing to do with whether the core product works, and Fathom's own version of it (a hard, unskippable desktop-app-install wall on Windows, discovered the hard way in this session) is arguably a mistake worth *not* copying: it nearly blocked this entire research session, and a 24-hour build shouldn't force any native install just to see a dashboard.

**10. Team analytics, CRM/Deals sync, coaching metrics (talk-time %, monologue detection) — stub as a locked marketing screen, exactly like Fathom does.** This is genuinely the right call, not a shortcut: Fathom's own Team Calls and Deals tabs, observed live in this session, are *themselves* just paywall screens with a mocked-up product screenshot behind a "Start 14-Day Trial" button — there's no real functionality to reverse-engineer here even in the actual product for a personal-tier account. Building a convincing locked-state marketing screen costs a fraction of what real CRM field-sync or coaching analytics would cost, and signals "this exists, upgrade for it" exactly as effectively.

**Bottom line:** spend the 24 hours on the pipeline from *stored transcript* to *summary → action items → highlights → search → share*, backed by one real LLM integration, and fake the two hardest infrastructure problems (live meeting capture, and cross-meeting conversational search) with an upload-a-sample-recording entry point and a scripted/limited version of "ask across your meetings," respectively. That mirrors what Fathom's own product does at its rough edges — it fakes its own "try it without a real call" flow, and it fakes its own team/CRM features behind a paywall screen for anyone not already paying for them.
